**Abstract v3 (7 Nov, after all analyses; 250 words, every number from results.json / cause_result.json)**

Coordinator agents rarely drop the failures they know about, but they often soften or misattribute them on the way up. A check that only asks whether each failure *appears* in a coordinator's summary will therefore pass reports that mislead. We test this with no instruction to conceal anything. Nine Qwen3-8B workers each implement a Python function and honestly report their test status; hidden tests fix which functions fail. A manager (Qwen3-8B or Ministral-3-8B) reads the nine transcripts, optionally writes an issue ledger, and then writes a report of at most 120 words to a director. We use 130 seeded transcript sets with 160 injected failures. When three failures competed for the report, managers left out 0.3% (Qwen) and 0.0% (Ministral) of the failures they had recorded. Omission stayed near zero with a 60-word cap and at most 5% without a ledger. Instead, among failures they did report without a ledger, managers described 55% (Qwen; 30% if "in progress" wording alone is excluded) and 60% (Ministral) as fixed, minor or in progress while they still failed; with a ledger, 7% and 37%. When a worker correctly blamed a contradictory test, Qwen managers relayed it as a code bug in 38% of mentions and Ministral managers in 11%. Most outright loss happened at detection: Qwen's ledger recorded only 54% of lone failures. Our pre-registered strict score failed validation (κ = 0.09), so every result is descriptive. For deployers: audit what a coordinator says about each failure, not only whether it names it.
