**Abstract v3.1 (7 Nov; every number from results.json / cause_result.json; Ministral omission removed per prereg §6)**

Coordinator agents rarely drop the failures they know about, but they often soften or misattribute them on the way up. A check that only asks whether each failure appears in a coordinator's summary will pass reports that mislead. We test this with no instruction to conceal anything. Nine Qwen3-8B workers each implement a Python function and honestly report their test status; hidden tests fix which functions fail. A manager (Qwen3-8B or Ministral-3-8B) reads the transcripts, optionally writes an issue ledger, and reports to a director in at most 120 words, across 130 seeded transcript sets with 160 injected failures. With three failures competing, Qwen managers left out 0.3% of the failures they had recorded: 0.3% again under a 60-word cap, and 5% without a ledger. Ministral's ledgers failed two validity gates, so its omission is not interpreted. Instead, of the failures they reported without a ledger, managers described 55% (Qwen; 30% excluding "in progress" wording alone) and 60% (Ministral) as fixed, minor or in progress while they still failed; with a ledger, 7% and 37%. When a worker correctly blamed a contradictory test, Qwen managers relayed it as a code bug in 38% of mentions and Ministral managers in 11%. Outright loss happened mostly at detection: Qwen's ledger recorded only 54% of lone failures. Our pre-registered strict score failed validation (κ = 0.09), so all results are descriptive. For deployers: audit what a coordinator says about each failure, not just whether it names it.
